# What performs

Read this before choosing ideas, hooks and lengths. It comes from the LuluBlox YT and BRUNOBLOX Studio analytics of 2026-11-02, public Roblox Shorts and web research. The sample is small, so each section says what is measured and what is only a hypothesis. Repeat the analysis every month or so (last section) and update this file.

- The storm video was published on 10-16 and took off only around 10-23, peaking at about 280k views a day on 10-27. **A video can take a week to start.**
- Its traffic: 94.2% Shorts feed, 3.6% search, 1.7% suggested. 99.6% were new viewers. Audience: 65% female, 74% aged 25–44 (parent accounts of kids watching), Philippines 25.6%, US 23.7%. Mobile 73%, tablet 18.5%, TV 5.5%.
- It was labelled **made for kids**, so that label does not stop a video from going viral.
- Median views: 1.5k for the kids-labelled batch of 10-16 to 10-22 (17–37 s), 11k for the not-for-kids batch of 10-23 to 10-31 (mostly 11–15 s).

**Do not read that last line as proof.** The label, the length, the hook style and the storm's boost to the channel all changed in the same week. Web evidence on the label is mixed too (see Sources). Compare only videos published on the same day with the same label, and judge a video only after 7 days.

## Hook: what the first line does

Measured, on both channels: the videos with the most views open on a concrete situation the viewer sees at once.

- Strong: "Oh no! It's raining!" (1.47M), "Spicy pepper challenge!" (31k), "I built a robot!" (25k), "Snowball fight!" (24k), "A ghost!" (16k). On BRUNOBLOX: "The ball fell into the neighbor's yard!" (89k), "Help! The sun is melting my snowman!" (73k).
- Weak: "Which one works?" (2.9k), "A, B, or secret?" (6k), "Ten seconds. Easy obby." (4.9k), "One coin. One try." (5k), "New hair day!" (9.3k), "So sleepy..." (12k).

Rules:

1. The first line names something **happening now** or an object already on screen: a problem, a discovery or a challenge against something absurd.
2. Do not open the voice with an abstract comparison ("Which X is the Y?"). Put the question second, once there is a reason to care: "Oh no! It's raining! Which umbrella works?".
3. `WHICH…` is fine in the **title**. On BRUNOBLOX the "QUAL…" titles do as well as the rest (median 24k vs 22k). The problem is a question with nothing at stake, not the word.
4. A strong hook alone is not enough: "I'm making the longest domino in the world!" got 6.7k on BRUNOBLOX. The topic counts too (see Ideas).

## First frame

Hypothesis from comparing frames at t=0.2 s and t=1.5 s of 13 strong and weak videos: strong openings are a **medium shot with the joke object and a readable sign in frame** (SPICY CHALLENGE, LULU LAB, a snowman, a robot). Weak ones are a big face close-up with no object. The viewer in the feed decides before the second line.

- Frame 1 shows Lulu, the object and, when it helps, a short sign. The close-up comes after.
- No establishing shot, no walking in, no empty sky.

## Stayed to watch is the number to watch

"Stayed to watch" (the percentage who did not swipe away) mostly follows views: 69–75% gave 22–31k, 47–58% gave 1–12k. There are exceptions (Pizza 63% with 28k, the storm 65%, and a BRUNOBLOX video with 70% and only 2k), so it is a signal, not a guarantee. Target **≥ 70%**. Below 60%, look at the first two seconds first.

Average viewed is a poor comparison across lengths: 13 s videos loop to 140%, while the 30 s storm video reached 86% and earned far more. Creator-reported targets are 60–80% viewed, but no official benchmark exists: compare with this channel's own videos.

## Length

- Measured: 13–15 s loops well but earns about 1.1 subs per 1,000 views; the storm video earned 1.8 at 30 s.
- On BRUNOBLOX (37 Roblox videos), median views by length: 20–29 s 23k, 30–39 s **34k**, 40–49 s 23k, 50 s or more 9k. Subs per 1,000 views: 3.1, 3.3, 4.7, 4.2. One 19 s video had 0.7.
- Outside data agrees: 25–35 s clusters among top performers, and nearly half of viral Shorts run 20–40 s (Toptal).

**Target 20–30 s, never under 15 s or over 40 s unless the user asks.** Lulu's own 16–37 s videos were all in the weak kids batch, so this is not proven for her: the first batch must mix lengths and be compared (below).

Credits: the 82-video batch used about 8,300 credits at 11.3 s each, about 9 credits per finished second. A 25 s video costs ~225 credits, so 10,000 credits make ~44 videos. Tell the user when a batch would not fit, and offer fewer videos before shorter ones.

## Ideas

Measured on both channels, topics that win are **universal childhood situations with no explanation needed**: rain, a ball over the fence, hide and seek with the dog, a melting snowman, a sandcastle against the wave, an alarm clock, an apple out of reach, spicy food, a snowball fight.

Topics that lose: adult subjects (a new sports car 6.8k), school-project setups (science fair volcano 5.4k) and memes and slang (brainrot and aura videos under 10k). Pure comparisons with nothing at stake (magnets, swings, balls) also did badly, but all of those were in the weak kids batch, so this one is less certain.

Formats that hit tens of millions on other channels, adapted to Lulu with **original stories and no third-party characters**:

| Format | Example seen | For Lulu |
|---|---|---|
| "When…" relatable moment | "When Noob received the test paper" 32M | "When your mom finds the cookie crumbs" |
| Sibling or family conflict | "Siblings fight" 19M, "Quando você trola demais o seu irmão" 55M | A little brother, a mom, a pet (blocky and original) |
| School or home drama | "Hiding the test result from mom" 55M | A bad grade, a broken vase, a lost tooth |
| X vs Y | "Noob vs Cactus" 4.3M | "Lulu vs the storm" is already this |
| World with a strange rule | "In this city nobody steps on the ground" 16M | "In this town it rains candy" |
| Parts | "PT1" / "PT2" 45M / 55M | Only when the user asks for a sequel (originality rule) |

These are hypotheses for us until tested. Characters from other games and trending licensed worlds (Brookhaven, 99 Nights and similar) drive many of those views, and the content rules forbid them.

Audience note: the Philippines and the US lead, so use simple English and avoid jokes that need American culture.

## Title, description and tags

Measured: **tags and descriptions do not explain the difference.** Strong and weak videos share the same tags (11 of 14 recurring tags on Lulu, all 11 on BRUNOBLOX), the same number of tags (17–18) and the same description length. With 94% of views from the feed and 3.6% from search, they cannot be what moves a video.

- Keep the `lote.py metadados` template and spend no extra time on tags.
- Title pattern of the top videos: first person plus a hidden result: `I ATE THE SPICIEST PEPPER IN ROBLOX...`, `I BUILT A ROBOT FRIEND IN ROBLOX...`. `LULU VS THE STORM IN ROBLOX! WHICH UMBRELLA WORKS?!` adds a conflict.
- The first description line repeats the hook, since it is what shows in the feed.
- `#Shorts` is not needed for distribution. Big channels use 2–4 hashtags and short, natural titles. Worth a test on a few videos, not a rule: all our titles are capitals, so we have no data of our own.

## Testing in each batch

1. Change one thing per batch and publish the two versions on the same days, at the same hours, with the same label.
2. Good first tests: 13–15 s vs 22–30 s; object-first frame vs face close-up.
3. After 7 days compare stayed to watch, views and subs per 1,000 views. Write the result here.

## How to read the numbers

- Table of all videos, in Advanced mode: `https://studio.youtube.com/channel/ID/analytics/tab-overview/period-default/explore?entity_type=CHANNEL&entity_id=ID&time_period=4_weeks&explore_type=TABLE_AND_CHART&metric=VIEWS&granularity=DAY&t_metrics=VIEWS&t_metrics=WATCH_TIME&t_metrics=AVERAGE_WATCH_TIME&t_metrics=AVERAGE_WATCH_PERCENTAGE&t_metrics=SUBSCRIBERS_NET_CHANGE&t_metrics=RATINGS_LIKES&dimension=VIDEO&o_column=VIEWS&o_direction=ANALYTICS_ORDER_DIRECTION_DESC` (rows `yta-explore-table-row`; `time_period=lifetime` for all time).
- One video: `https://studio.youtube.com/video/ID/analytics/tab-overview/period-default`, with `tab-reach_viewers`, `tab-interest_viewers` ("Continuaram assistindo" / "Stayed to watch") and `tab-build_audience`.
- The Claude in Chrome output cuts off at about 1,300 characters and refuses URLs with query strings, so send big tables to a small local receiver with `fetch`. Stop that receiver afterwards: port 8768 belongs to `servidor_envio.py`.
- The Chrome account must own the channel. LuluBlox YT and BRUNOBLOX are on different Google accounts: switch with the avatar menu, not the button on the "Ops" page.
